**[DRAFT — EN]**

**The MICE industry's biggest lie: "A great venue makes a great event."**

After producing 1,500+ events in 130+ destinations — I'm here to tell you: the venue is the last thing that matters.

Here's what actually drives event ROI in 2026:

**The Brief (40% of outcome)**
A bad brief kills even the most spectacular venue. I've watched $500K galas fall flat because nobody asked: "What should participants *feel* on the way home?"

**The Narrative (30%)**
Events are stories. They have an opening, a turning point, and a resolution. When the content arc is missing — even the best production feels hollow.

**The People Moments (20%)**
The conversations that happen between sessions. The shared dinner on night two. The "I didn't expect to meet *you* here" moment. These are what participants remember.

**The Logistics (10%)**
Yes — the venue, the AV, the catering. Critical when they fail. Invisible when they work.

Most companies allocate their evaluation energy exactly backwards.

The shift I'm seeing with the smartest CMOs and HR leaders right now?
They're not asking "what's the best venue in [city]?"

They're asking: **"What's the experience we want people to carry back to work on Monday morning?"**

That's the question that separates good events from ones that move the needle.

**[DRAFT — EN]**

**Most companies brief their event production partner the same way they order office supplies.**

SKU. Quantity. Budget. Deadline.

And then wonder why the event felt transactional.

After 1,500+ productions across Israel, Europe, and 130+ destinations — here's the briefing framework that separates high-impact events from forgettable ones:

**The 5 Questions Worth 80% of Your Event Outcome:**

**1. What behaviour should this event change?**
Not "what should people learn" — what should they *do differently* on day one back at the office?

**2. Who in the room needs to feel seen?**
Your top performers. Your newly promoted managers. The team that survived the hard year. Name them. Design for them.

**3. What's the "water cooler story" you want circulating the week after?**
Every great event produces one. Plan it in advance.

**4. What does success look like for the CEO at 11pm on event night?**
And for the CMO? And for HR? Align these before you sign anything.

**5. What's the one thing you've always wanted to do at an event — but thought was "too much"?**
That's usually the thing worth doing.
