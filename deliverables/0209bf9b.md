**📋 English version (ready to post):**

> 5 questions every HR leader should ask before choosing an event production company.
>
> (Most companies only ask one.)
>
> 1️⃣ **"How much will this burden my team?"**
> If the answer is "minimal" — that's a production company selling labor, not a solution.
>
> 2️⃣ **"Who is my point of contact on event day?"**
> Not about the name — about who has decision-making authority in the field.
>
> 3️⃣ **"Can I speak with a client from last year — not from your success stories page?"**
> The clients a company doesn't feature are the real reference.
>
> 4️⃣ **"Tell me about something that went wrong at your last event."**
> Everyone prepares perfectly. The question is how they manage crises.
>
> 5️⃣ **"What ROI will you help me measure?"**
> An event with no data is an expense, not an investment.

> → לקוח מקבל briefing של 30 דקות — רק highlights, לא פרטים

> → Contingency scenarios מועברים לכל אחד בצוות
>

> → Wake-up call פנימי ב-05:30
> → Site ready ב-06:30 — 3 שעות לפני פתיחה
>
> זאת ההבדל בין "אירגנו כנס" ל"הפקנו חוויה".

>
> \#EventProduction \#BehindTheScenes \#CorporateEvents

---

**📋 English version (ready to post):**

> What happens in the 72 hours before a 500-person conference — from the inside.
>
> 72 hours out:
> → Full site walkthrough with the venue event manager
> → Emergency drill: what happens if the keynote speaker cancels?
> → Tech equipment check: AV, lighting, generator backups
>
> 48 hours out:
> → Print and pack all participant materials
> → Full dry-run of all sessions with the tech crew
> → WhatsApp group with all vendors — tested
>
> 24 hours out:
> → Client gets a 30-min briefing — highlights only, no details
> → Our team is on-site — not at home
> → Contingency scenarios shared with every team member
>
> D-Day:
> → Internal wake-up at 05:30
> → Site ready at 06:30 — 3 hours before doors open
>
> That's the difference between "we organized a conference" and "we produced an experience."
> The client sees the circus. We run the circus.
>
